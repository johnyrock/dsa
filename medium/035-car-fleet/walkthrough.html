Medium · Stack · LeetCode #853

Car Fleet, narrated

I will first merge fleets through repeated passes over their arrival times, then show the single backward pass that does the same work directly.

Pattern used Stack sort front to back; a car slower than the fleet ahead starts a new fleet, otherwise it merges

STEP 1

The problem

Cars drive along a single lane toward target. Car i starts at position[i] and moves at speed[i]. Nobody can overtake: when a faster car reaches the bumper of a slower one it drops to that speed, and from then on the two are one fleet. Count the fleets that cross the target line.

Running example: target = 12, position = [10, 8, 0, 5, 3], speed = [2, 4, 1, 1, 3]. The answer is 3.

Work it by hand. The car at 10 needs (12 − 10) / 2 = 1 hour; the car at 8 needs (12 − 8) / 4 = 1 hour too, so they arrive together and count as one fleet. The car at 5 crawls at speed 1 and needs 7 hours; the car at 3 at speed 3 would need only 3, so it catches the car at 5 and is stuck behind it: one fleet. The car at 0 needs 12 hours and never reaches anyone. Three fleets.

Note the rule "a car that catches another at the target still counts as the same fleet". Equal arrival times mean one fleet.

STEP 2

First instinct: repeatedly merge adjacent arrival times

I can sort cars from front to back, calculate their independent arrival times, and repeatedly merge a car into the fleet ahead whenever it would arrive no later.

def carFleet(target, position, speed):    times = [(target - p) / s for p, s in sorted(zip(position, speed), reverse=True)]    changed = True    while changed:        changed = False        for i in range(1, len(times)):            if times[i] <= times[i - 1]:                times.pop(i)                changed = True                break    return len(times)

This is correct: a car that would arrive earlier catches the fleet in front and shares its later arrival time. Sorting costs O(n log n), and repeated scans and removals make the total time O(n²). The arrival-time list uses O(n) space.

I decide each merge while I move from the frontmost car backward. Keeping only the arrival times of fleets I have already formed removes the repeated passes.

STEP 3

Key insight: compare arrival times, front to back

Ignore the other cars for a moment and compute, for each car, how long it would I take on an empty road: time = (target − pos) / spd.

I look at two cars where A is ahead of B. If B's time is less than or equal to A's, B would reach the target no later than A, which is impossible without passing, so B must catch A before the target and merge into A's fleet. That fleet then arrives at A's time, because the leader sets the pace. If B's time is greater, B never catches A and is its own fleet.

A car's fate depends only on the fleet directly ahead of it, and that fleet's time is the time of its slowest, front-most leader. I process cars from the front, and each car only has to look at the most recent fleet.

"Most recent fleet" is a stack top. I push a time when a new fleet starts; when a car merges, I push nothing, because the fleet's time is already there.

STEP 4

The approach, line by line

  • cars = sorted(zip(position, speed), reverse=True): pair each position with its speed and order by position, closest to the target first.
  • fleets = []: a stack of arrival times, one per fleet, front-most at the bottom.
  • For each car, time = (target − pos) / spd, as a float.
  • if not fleets or time > fleets[-1]: slower than the fleet ahead (or no fleet ahead), so I push a new fleet.
  • Otherwise do nothing: the car catches the fleet on top and rides with it.
  • return len(fleets).

Sorted from the front, the running example is (10, 2), (8, 4), (5, 1), (3, 3), (0, 1) with times 1, 1, 7, 3, 12.

STEP 5

Trace it: the cars at 10 and 8

Car at 10, speed 2: time = (12 − 10) / 2 = 1.0. The stack is empty, so this is the first fleet. I push 1.0.

Car at 8, speed 4: time = (12 − 8) / 4 = 1.0. I compare with the top: 1.0 > 1.0 is false. This car would arrive at the same moment as the fleet ahead, which means it reaches that fleet exactly at the target line, and by the rules that is the same fleet. I push nothing.

The stack is still [1.0]. One fleet so far, two cars in it.

STEP 6

Trace it: the cars at 5 and 3

Car at 5, speed 1: time = (12 − 5) / 1 = 7.0. Top is 1.0, and 7.0 > 1.0, so it never catches the leaders. I push 7.0: a second fleet.

Car at 3, speed 3: time = (12 − 3) / 3 = 3.0. Top is now 7.0, and 3.0 > 7.0 is false. On an empty road this car would arrive four hours earlier than the crawler at 5, so it is certain to catch it, and from then on it crawls too. I push nothing.

I see that the merged fleet's time stays 7.0, the leader's time, not 3.0. That is exactly why nothing is pushed or replaced: the stack already holds the right number.

STEP 7

Trace it: the car at 0, and the answer

Car at 0, speed 1: time = (12 − 0) / 1 = 12.0. Top is 7.0, and 12.0 > 7.0. It is slower than everything ahead and never catches up. I push 12.0.

The loop ends with fleets = [1.0, 7.0, 12.0], and len(fleets) is 3.

The stack was never popped. Every car either starts a fleet (push) or joins the one on top (no-op). That is what makes the pass after sorting a single linear sweep.
STEP 8

Where this goes wrong: the comparison, the sort order, and integer division

>= instead of >. The car at 8 has time = 1.0, equal to the fleet ahead. With time >= fleets[-1] it is pushed as its own fleet and the function returns 4 instead of 3. Equal times meet at the line and are one fleet.

Sorting ascending. I drop reverse=True and the first car processed is the one at 0 with time 12.0. Every later car has a smaller time, nothing is ever pushed again, and the answer is 1. Each car must be compared with the car ahead, so the front car has to go first.

Integer division. With (target − pos) // spd, target = 10, cars at 8 (speed 2) and 6 (speed 3) both get time 1: the true times are 1.0 and 1.33, so they never meet, but the rounding merges them and returns 1 instead of 2.

I sort descending by position, I compare with strict >, and keep the time as a float.
STEP 9

Time and space

The sort dominates: O(n log n). The sweep afterwards does one division and one comparison per car, and the stack is only ever pushed, so it is O(n).

ApproachTimeSpace
Tick-by-tick simulationO(n · target / dt)O(n)
Sort + stack of fleet timesO(n log n)O(n)
Sort + one float and a counterO(n log n)O(n) for the sort

Because the stack is never popped, only its top is ever read, so it can be replaced by a single variable holding the slowest time seen so far and an integer counter. The stack version is kept because it says what is being counted.

STEP 10

The final solution

Six lines. Every one of them maps to a step above.

  • sorted(zip(position, speed), reverse=True): front car first, speeds kept attached.
  • fleets = []: one arrival time per fleet.
  • time = (target - pos) / spd: the empty-road arrival time, as a float.
  • if not fleets or time > fleets[-1]: slower than the fleet ahead means a new fleet.
  • fleets.append(time) only in that case; a merging car pushes nothing.
  • return len(fleets).
One-line summary to remember: I walk the cars from the front; a car that would arrive later than the fleet ahead is a new fleet, anything else merges.
