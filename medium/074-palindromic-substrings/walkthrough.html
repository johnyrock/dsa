Palindromic Substrings, narrated

Scroll through the thinking, from testing every substring to expanding outward from each of the 2n − 1 centres and counting one palindrome per matching step, with the state of the algorithm shown alongside.

Pattern used Dynamic Programming a palindrome extends the smaller palindrome inside it, so grow outward from every centre and count each step

STEP 1

The problem

You get a string s. Count its palindromic substrings: contiguous pieces that read the same forwards and backwards. Two substrings at different positions count separately even when their letters are identical.

Running example: s = "aaa". The answer is 6: three single "a"s, two "aa"s (positions 0–1 and 1–2), and one "aaa".

Every single character is a palindrome, so the answer is at least len(s). The work is in finding the longer ones without testing every slice from scratch.

STEP 2

First instinct: test every substring

For every start i and every end j, slice, reverse, compare, and bump a counter when they match.

total = 0for i in range(n):    for j in range(i, n):        sub = s[i:j + 1]        if sub == sub[::-1]:            total += 1

There are about n² / 2 substrings and each reversal is O(n), so this is O(n³). On "aaa" it checks 6 slices and all 6 pass, which is fine; at n = 1000 it is half a billion character comparisons, most of them re-examining the same middle letters.

STEP 3

Reframe: a palindrome is a smaller palindrome plus matching ends

If s[left+1 .. right-1] is a palindrome and s[left] == s[right], then s[left .. right] is a palindrome too. That is a recurrence: the answer for a wider slice is decided by the slice just inside it. The brute force ignores it and re-reads the inside every time.

Every palindrome has a middle. For odd length it is a character ("aaa" is centred on index 1); for even length it is the gap between two characters ("aa" at 0–1 is centred on the gap after index 0). A string of length n has n character-centres and n − 1 gap-centres.

2n − 1 centres, and each palindrome belongs to exactly one of them. Count from the centres and nothing is counted twice.
STEP 4

The key idea: expand and count every matching step

For each centre, run two expansions: left = right = centre for odd lengths and left = centre, right = centre + 1 for even lengths. While both pointers are in bounds and s[left] == s[right], the slice between them is a palindrome, so do total += 1, then step left -= 1 and right += 1.

  • for centre in range(len(s)) visits every character.
  • for left, right in ((centre, centre), (centre, centre + 1)) tries the odd and the even centre at that position.
  • while left >= 0 and right < len(s) and s[left] == s[right] expands, bounds checked first.
  • total += 1 inside the loop: one palindrome per successful widening.

Compared with Longest Palindromic Substring, the only change is what happens on a match: count it instead of comparing lengths.

STEP 5

Trace it: centre 0

Odd run from index 0: left = right = 0, and s[0] == s[0] trivially, so total = 1 for the single "a". Step out to left = −1, out of bounds, stop.

Even run: left = 0, right = 1. s[0] = a and s[1] = a match, so total = 2 for "aa" at 0–1. Step out to left = −1, right = 2, stop on the bound check.

Two palindromes from the first position, one from each kind of centre.

STEP 6

Trace it: centre 1, the middle

Odd run from index 1: s[1] == s[1], total = 3 for the middle "a". Widen to left = 0, right = 2: s[0] = a, s[2] = a, match, total = 4 for "aaa". Widen to left = −1, right = 3, both out of bounds, stop.

Even run: left = 1, right = 2. s[1] = a, s[2] = a, match, total = 5 for "aa" at 1–2. Widen to left = 0, right = 3; right is out, stop.

The centre at index 1 alone produced three palindromes, which is why the counter has to live inside the while loop.

STEP 7

Trace it: centre 2, the answer

Odd run from index 2: total = 6 for the last "a". Widen to left = 1, right = 3; right is out of bounds, stop.

Even run: left = 2, right = 3. right < len(s) fails immediately, so nothing is counted; there is no gap after the last character.

Return 6. Three centres, five expansions that found something, six matching steps, six palindromes.
STEP 8

Where this goes wrong: even centres and where the counter lives

Skipping the even expansion. Write for left, right in ((centre, centre),) and every even-length palindrome vanishes. On "aaa" the two "aa"s are lost and the result is 4, not 6. The panel shows that run: only the character-centres are ever lit, the gaps never are.

Counting once per centre. Move total += 1 below the while loop (guarded by "did it expand at all") and each centre contributes at most one. On "aaa" the middle centre finds both "a" and "aaa" but records one, giving 5.

Comparing before the bounds check. while s[left] == s[right] and left >= 0 ... reads s[-1], which in Python is the last character, and raises IndexError when right runs off the end. Bounds first.

Two runs per index, and count inside the loop. Every step outward that still matches is one more palindrome.
STEP 9

Time and space

2n − 1 centres, each expanding at most n/2 steps: O(n²) time. A counter and two pointers: O(1) space.

ApproachTimeSpace
Every substring, reverse eachO(n³)O(1)
DP table is_pal[i][j]O(n²)O(n²)
Expand around centreO(n²)O(1)
Manacher's algorithmO(n)O(n)

The 2-D table stores the same "inside is a palindrome, ends match" recurrence explicitly; expanding from the centre evaluates it in the order that needs no storage.

STEP 10

The final solution

Nine lines. Every one of them maps to a step above.

  • total = 0 is the running count.
  • for centre in range(len(s)) visits every character-centre.
  • for left, right in ((centre, centre), (centre, centre + 1)) runs the odd and even expansions.
  • while left >= 0 and right < len(s) and s[left] == s[right] widens while the ends match, bounds first.
  • total += 1 counts the palindrome s[left:right + 1] just confirmed.
  • left -= 1 and right += 1 step outward.
  • return total.
One-line summary to remember: every palindrome has one centre; expand from all 2n − 1 of them and count each matching step.
