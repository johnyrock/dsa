Surrounded Regions, narrated

I will first test whether every O can reach the border, then mark the border-connected regions before flipping the rest.

Pattern used Graph Traversal flood-fill from every border O, then flip the unreached O's

STEP 1

The problem

You get a board of 'X' and 'O' cells. A region is a group of 'O' cells connected up, down, left or right. A region is surrounded when it is fenced in by 'X' on every side, which is the same thing as saying that none of its cells sits on the border of the board.

Capture every surrounded region: flip all of its cells to 'X', in place. Regions that touch the border stay exactly as they are. Nothing is returned; the board itself is the answer.

Running example: a 4 × 5 board with three 'O' regions. The blob at (1,1), (1,2), (2,2) touches no edge, so it is captured. The pair (1,4), (2,4) sits on the right edge and the single (3,1) sits on the bottom edge, so both survive.

Notice the definition is about the whole region, not individual cells. (1,1) is not on the border and has an 'X' above and to its left, but that alone does not decide anything; what matters is whether any cell it can reach through 'O' neighbours is on the border.

STEP 2

First instinct: ask each cell whether it can escape

I can run a fresh breadth-first search from each O and flip it only when that search cannot reach the border.

for r in range(rows):    for c in range(cols):        if board[r][c] == "O" and not reaches_border(board, r, c):            to_flip.append((r, c))for r, c in to_flip: board[r][c] = "X"

This is correct when reaches_border runs BFS through O cells. Repeating that BFS makes the time complexity O((m · n)²), and its queue and visited set use O(m · n) space.

Starting with the only cells guaranteed safe—border Os—means I mark every escape route once.

STEP 3

Reframe: the border decides everything

Flip the question around. Instead of "which regions are surrounded?", ask "which regions are safe?" A region is safe exactly when it contains at least one border cell. And every safe region is reachable by a flood fill that starts at a border 'O'.

Flood-fill from every 'O' on the four edges. Everything those fills reach is safe. Everything they do not reach is, by definition, surrounded.

That removes the decision from the traversal entirely. There is no "did I touch the border?" flag to track, and no undo, because the fill only ever runs over cells that are already known to be safe. The surrounded regions never get traversed at all; they are identified by omission in a plain scan afterwards.

The border of a 4 × 5 board has 14 cells. Only three of them are 'O': (1,4), (2,4) and (3,1). Those three seeds are the whole starting point.

STEP 4

The key idea: mark safe with a third symbol, then sweep

The fill needs a way to remember what it has already reached, and the board has only two symbols. Introduce a third one, 'T', meaning "an 'O' that is known to be safe". The algorithm becomes:

  • In mark_safe(r, c), I push (r, c), mark it 'T', and mark each eligible neighbour before adding it to the stack.
  • Seed it from the left and right columns: for c in (0, cols - 1) across every row.
  • Seed it from the top and bottom rows: for r in (0, rows - 1) across every column. A corner is visited by both loops, but after the first visit it is 'T', not 'O', so the second visit is a no-op.
  • Sweep the whole board once: 'O' becomes 'X' (surrounded), 'T' becomes 'O' (safe, restored).

The explicit stack rather than recursion matters at the limits: a 200 × 200 board of all 'O' is one region 40,000 cells deep, well past Python's recursion limit.

STEP 5

Trace it: the right edge

The first seeding loop walks rows 0 to 3 and looks at column 0 and column 4 in each. Column 0 is all 'X'. In column 4, row 0 is 'X', and row 1 is 'O': call mark_safe(1, 4).

Inside, I mark (1,4) as 'T' and add it to the stack. Its only live neighbour is (2,4), so I mark and add that cell too. When I process (2,4), every neighbour is blocked, visited, or out of bounds, and the stack empties.

The seeding loop continues to row 2, column 4 and finds 'T' there, not 'O', so it does not start a second fill for the same region. Two cells marked safe so far.

STEP 6

Trace it: the bottom edge

The second seeding loop walks columns 0 to 4 and looks at row 0 and row 3 in each. Row 0 is all 'X'. In row 3, column 1 is 'O': call mark_safe(3, 1).

I set (3,1) to 'T', push it, then pop it. Its neighbours are (4,1) out of bounds, (2,1) which is 'X', (3,2) which is 'X', and (3,0) which is 'X', so there is nothing to push. Three cells are marked 'T' in total, and the interior blob at (1,1), (1,2), (2,2) has never been touched. It is still plain 'O', which is exactly the signal the sweep will use.

The fills only ever visit safe cells. The surrounded blob is identified by never being visited.
STEP 7

Trace it: the sweep

One final pass over all 20 cells. (1,1) is 'O', so it becomes 'X'; likewise (1,2) and (2,2). That is the capture: 3 cells flipped. (1,4), (2,4) and (3,1) are 'T', so they become 'O' again. Every other cell is 'X' and is left alone.

After the sweep no 'T' remains, so the caller never sees the temporary symbol. The board now reads: row 1 is X X X X O, row 2 is X X X X O, row 3 is X O X X X. That matches the expected output.

STEP 8

Where this goes wrong: seeding half the border

Two specific bugs live here, and both pass a casual read.

Seeding only the top row and the left column. Writing for c in range(cols): if board[0][c] == "O" and the matching board[r][0] loop, and forgetting rows - 1 and cols - 1, misses every 'O' on the bottom and right edges. On the running example (1,4), (2,4) and (3,1) are never marked, the sweep flips them along with the blob, and the whole board comes out 'X': 0 cells kept instead of 3.

Marking reached cells as 'O' instead of 'T'. Then the fill cannot tell visited from unvisited. Popping (1,4) pushes (2,4); popping (2,4) sees (1,4) still 'O' and pushes it back. The two cells ping-pong forever on any safe region with two or more cells.

Seed from all four edges, using 0 and rows - 1 for rows, 0 and cols - 1 for columns. And always mark with a symbol that the neighbour check will not match again.
STEP 9

Time and space

The seeding loops look at the 2 × (m + n) border cells. Across all fills, each 'O' is marked and pushed at most once, popped at most once, and each pop looks at four neighbours. The sweep touches every cell once. Total O(m × n) time.

The stack can hold a large fraction of one region in the worst case, so O(m × n) space worst case. There is no visited set: the board's own 'T' symbol records what has been reached.

Fresh BFS from every O cellO((m × n)²)O(m × n) per search
Fill each region, collect cells, flip if no border hitO(m × n)O(m × n) list + visited set

STEP 10

The final solution

Every line maps to a step above.

  • rows, cols = len(board), len(board[0]) caches the dimensions for the bounds check.
  • mark_safe(r, c) is my flood fill: I mark safe cells as 'T' before adding them to the stack.
  • for c in (0, cols - 1) inside for r in range(rows) seeds from the left and right columns.
  • for r in (0, rows - 1) inside for c in range(cols) seeds from the top and bottom rows.
  • The sweep: if board[r][c] == "O": board[r][c] = "X" captures, elif board[r][c] == "T": board[r][c] = "O" restores.
My rule is: I flood-fill from the border and mark what I reach; any O still present afterwards is surrounded.
