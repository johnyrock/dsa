Medium · Dynamic Programming · LeetCode #309

Best Time to Buy and Sell Stock with Cooldown, narrated

I track the best balance for holding, selling, and resting at the end of every day.

Pattern used Dynamic Programming state machine: hold / sold / rest as three rolling variables

STEP 1

The problem

You get the price of one stock for each day. You can buy and sell as many times as you like, but you may hold at most one share at a time, and the day after you sell is a mandatory cooldown: you cannot buy on it.

Return the largest profit you can end with. Buying costs the day's price, selling earns it, and you start with no shares.

Running example: prices = [1, 2, 3, 0, 2]. The answer is 3: buy at 1, sell at 2, rest on the day of the 3, buy at 0, sell at 2. That is (2 − 1) + (2 − 0) = 3.

Without the cooldown you would sell at 3 instead of 2 and make 4. The cooldown is what makes the greedy "sell at every local peak" answer wrong: selling at 3 on day 2 forbids buying the 0 on day 3.

STEP 2

First instinct: try every plan

My first instinct is to try every choice on each day: I rest, buy when I am allowed, or sell when I hold a share, then keep the best result.

def best(i, holding):    if i >= len(prices):        return 0    skip = best(i + 1, holding)    if holding:        return max(skip, prices[i] + best(i + 2, False))  # sell, then cooldown    return max(skip, -prices[i] + best(i + 1, True))      # buy

The i + 2 call handles the cooldown, but the branches take O(2ⁿ) time and O(n) space for the call stack. At 5000 days, it never finishes.

The same (i, holding) state appears along many plans. I only need the best balance for each kind of end-of-day state.

STEP 3

The key idea: three states, rolled forward

At the end of any day, I am in exactly one of three situations, and only the best cash balance for each matters:

  • hold — I own a share. The cash is negative if I have not sold yet.
  • sold — I sold today. Tomorrow is the cooldown.
  • rest — I own nothing and am free to buy tomorrow.

Each day, every state is rebuilt from yesterday's three values:

  • hold = max(hold, rest - price) — keep holding, or buy today. Buying is only allowed from rest. That one detail is the cooldown rule.
  • sold = hold + price — the only way to have just sold is to have been holding.
  • rest = max(rest, sold) — I keep resting, or I sold yesterday and today is the cooldown.
The exponential tree collapses to three numbers per day because a plan's past does not matter; I only need its ending state and cash balance.
STEP 4

The approach, line by line

  • hold, sold, rest = float("-inf"), 0, 0 seeds the states before day 0. I cannot be holding a share I never bought, so hold starts impossible; doing nothing leaves 0 cash in the other two.
  • for price in prices walks the days in order.
  • hold, sold, rest = max(hold, rest - price), hold + price, max(rest, sold) is the whole recurrence in one tuple assignment. The right-hand side is evaluated with yesterday's values before any name is rebound.
  • return max(sold, rest) — ending while holding a share is never better than not having bought it, so only the two cash-only states compete.
STEP 5

Trace it: days 0 and 1

Day 0, price = 1. hold = max(-inf, 0 − 1) = −1: buying today is my only way to hold. sold = −inf + 1 = −inf: I cannot sell what I never had. rest = max(0, 0) = 0.

Day 1, price = 2. hold = max(−1, 0 − 2) = −1: I retain the share bought at 1 instead of buying at 2. sold = −1 + 2 = 1: I can sell that share for a profit of 1. rest = max(0, −inf) = 0.

After two days the best "just sold" plan is worth 1 and the best "holding" plan is still the share bought at 1.

STEP 6

Trace it: days 2 and 3, the cooldown bites

Day 2, price = 3. hold = max(−1, 0 − 3) = −1. sold = −1 + 3 = 2: selling the share bought at 1 for 3. rest = max(0, 1) = 1: yesterday's sale at 2 is now a completed trade sitting in cash.

Day 3, price = 0. hold = max(−1, 1 − 0) = 1: buy the 0 from rest, which holds the 1 from selling at 2. The plan that sold at 3 is in sold = 2, and hold is not allowed to read from it; that is the cooldown. sold = −1 + 0 = −1. rest = max(1, 2) = 2: the sale at 3 finishes its cooldown and becomes available cash.

Both plans survive side by side: hold = 1 carries "sold at 2, bought at 0" and rest = 2 carries "sold at 3, still resting". Neither is discarded yet.
STEP 7

Trace it: day 4, the answer

Day 4, price = 2. hold = max(1, 2 − 2) = 1. sold = 1 + 2 = 3: sell the share bought at 0 for 2, on top of the 1 already banked. rest = max(2, −1) = 2.

The loop is over. max(sold, rest) = max(3, 2) = 3. The plan that sold at the peak of 3 ends with 2; the plan that sold early at 2, rested, and bought the dip at 0 ends with 3 and wins.

STEP 8

Where this goes wrong: reading today's values

Updating the states one line at a time. If I write the three updates as separate statements, hold = max(hold, rest - price), then sold = hold + price, then rest = max(rest, sold), sold reads the new hold. That lets a plan buy and sell on the same day for zero profit, and worse, rest then reads the new sold so the cooldown disappears entirely. On the running example it returns 4, not 3: I buy at 1, sell at 3, buy at 0, and sell at 2 with no rest day. The tuple assignment exists precisely so all three right-hand sides see yesterday's values.

Letting hold read from sold. hold = max(hold, max(rest, sold) - price) is the recurrence for the no-cooldown problem and also gives 4 here. The cooldown lives in that one missing read.

Seeding hold = 0. That pretends I start the first day holding a free share. [2, 1] then returns 2 instead of 0, because “selling” the phantom share on day 0 earns 2.

I compute all three states from yesterday's numbers in one tuple assignment, and only rest may buy. On [1, 2, 3, 0, 2], I get 3; any 4 means the cooldown leaked.
STEP 9

Time and space

One pass over the prices with three max and add operations per day: O(n) time. Three integers, no table: O(1) space.

ApproachTimeSpace
Plain recursion over daysO(2ⁿ)O(n) call stack
Memoized on (day, holding)O(n)O(n) cache + stack
Three-state table, one row per dayO(n)O(n)
Three rolling variablesO(n)O(1)

Each state only reads the previous day, so the per-day table collapses to three variables, exactly the way Climbing Stairs collapses to two.

STEP 10

The final solution

Five lines. Every one of them maps to a step above.

  • hold, sold, rest = float("-inf"), 0, 0 — no share yet, no cash moved.
  • for price in prices — one day at a time.
  • max(hold, rest - price) — keep holding, or buy from a rest day.
  • hold + price — the only way to have just sold.
  • max(rest, sold) — keep resting, or finish yesterday's cooldown.
  • return max(sold, rest) — never end holding a share.
One-line summary to remember: three states per day, hold / sold / rest, and buying is only allowed from rest.
